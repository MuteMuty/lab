Privacy

The lab counts a few things, so the night shift can see what people enjoy and what they actually do. This is all of it.

What's counted

  • Likes. How many times each project was liked. Pressing a heart keeps a receipt in your browser so you can take the like back. Besides that, only Afterstep keeps something in your browser: your progress (puzzles solved, hints seen, lessons done), so it remembers your results and streak.
  • Page views. One each time the lab's front page, Afterstep or Inkchime opens. They're the only pages counted so far.
  • Projects opened. Which project you open from the front page, once per project each time the page opens.
  • Puzzles started. In Afterstep, which puzzle you make a first move in: today's, a past one or a lesson. Once per puzzle each time the page opens.
  • Puzzles finished. In Afterstep, which puzzle you solve, about how many moves it took (a range, like 11–20) and whether you used a hint. Once per puzzle each time the page opens.
  • Songs started. In Inkchime, that you drew your first line or placed your first note, and whether the page began from a shared song link. Once each time the page opens.
  • Songs shared. In Inkchime, that you pressed Share and got a link, and whether the page began from a shared song link. Once each time the page opens.

Each one also carries what your browser tells any site: the page's address (without anything after a ? or #), the site you came from, and your browser, system, language, time zone and screen size.

How

  • No cookies, nothing saved in your browser for counting, and no accounts.
  • Counts are kept by PostHog in its EU cloud, which drops your IP address.
  • If your browser asks sites not to track you (Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control), nothing is counted except the likes you press.

Never sent

Anything you type, draw, record or upload, the moves you make in a puzzle, your drawing in Inkchime or its song link, any free text, or anything that identifies you. The one place your words are kept is the suggestion box: the night shift reads what you send, and it's never shown publicly.
